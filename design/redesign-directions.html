OpenStrap Edge · dark redesign · three directions sample data

Most dark health apps are near-black with one neon accent. Each direction here starts from something near a wrist sensor instead: a watch dial, a darkroom print, a terrain map.

Colour means the metricRecovery, strain, sleep and heart each keep one colour in every direction, so you can tell which is which before reading the label.
Missing shows as missingA day with no data shows "—" and says why. No ring, needle or bar ever shows a value that wasn't measured. See the "not scored" variant in each set.
Big type is for measured numbersOnly real readings get the large display face. Labels, captions and units stay small and quiet.
Same five tabsHome · Health · Nutrition · Workout · Wellness. The layout of the app doesn't change; its visual identity does.

Direction A · recommended

Horologe.

You wear this on your wrist, so it borrows from fine watchmaking. A smoked-green dial, gold-edged markers, glow-in-the-dark green for recovery, a red second hand for strain and blue steel for sleep. Readiness is the main dial. Sleep and strain are the small dials beside it. A live workout is a stopwatch. Big numbers use a high-contrast serif like a watch face. Labels are small, spaced-out capitals, like text engraved on a watch.

Direction B

Darkroom

The band records you in the dark, and a darkroom is where film gets developed. Each day is a frame on a strip of film you scroll through. Today's readiness is the print on cream paper. A month of data is a contact sheet. A live workout fills the screen with red safelight. Numbers use a tall condensed industrial font. The small codes in the corners use a dot-matrix font, like the text printed along the edge of film.

Direction C

Strata

Your body data drawn like terrain. Readiness is an island: each point of score lights up another contour line toward the peak. Sleep is a cross-section through the ground: the deeper the sleep, the deeper the layer. Trends pile up like rock layers. The colours are minerals: green copper for recovery, rust orange for strain, blue lapis for sleep, yellow sulphur for food, pink mineral for heart. Numbers use an extra-wide font that looks like the lettering on survey maps.

How they compare

A · HorologeB · DarkroomC · Strata
Feels likeA luxury watch. Calm and precise, made to last.A photo darkroom. Bold, gritty and full of personality.A field survey. Analytical, like reading a map.
Signature elementA dial with gold markers and a hand, plus small dials for sleep and strainA film-strip day picker and today's score as a printA contour-map island and a cross-section of sleep
Ages wellVery well. Watch-dial design has lasted a century.Well. It's a strong theme, so there's a small risk it feels like a costume.Well. The palette looks the most scientific.
Crowded screensLedger rows handle long lists cleanlyContact sheet grids fit a lot on screenLayer cards and sparklines, dense but clear
Build effort in FlutterMedium. Dial and small-dial painters, sunburst texture shaderMedium. Film strip, print card, dot-matrix fontHigher. Contour generation, cross-section painter
RiskSerif numbers can read "fashion" if overused. Keep them for measured values only.Red safelight and gritty textures can hurt readability. Needs care in dense screens.An extra-wide font takes a lot of horizontal space when text is enlarged.

Recommendation: A · Horologe. The band is worn on the wrist, so watch-dial design fits it best. Its structure (a main dial, small dials, a stopwatch for live workouts, a charge gauge for the band) maps directly onto what the app shows. It's the only direction where the metaphor helps you read the data instead of just decorating it. Parts can be mixed: B's contact sheet would work well as A's month view, for example.
